# Building and marking questions

## Interrogation questions (Part 1, recall, debrief)

- **Built from their page, not from the book.** Every question targets something their blank page or answer showed missing, wrong or shaky, or a reason they did not give. Never ask about what they already wrote correctly unless it is to probe the why.
- **The wording must not give the answer away.** "What is the order of basicity of the methylamines in water, and what decides it?" is a question. "Remember that the order in water is 2° > 1° > 3°; why?" is a statement.
- **Exam-relevant.** Ask about what boards and BITSAT test: exact formulae and conditions, every step of a derivation or mechanism, exceptions, and the reasoning behind trends.
- **One twist per chunk.** The same idea in a new situation: changed conditions, a different substrate, a limiting case.
- **Committed answers are hidden until after the attempt.** For a blank page and for interrogation questions, the `correctAnswer` is the fixed sentence given in the running instructions, never content. Keep the real answer key in mind and mark against it.

## Marking

- Marked strictly against the textbook. Plain-text maths and chemistry are fine (`n_e*n_h = n_i^2`, `CH3NH2 + HCl -> CH3NH3+ Cl-`).
- A near-miss is a miss: say exactly what was right and what was missing.
- Hint ladder: first miss gets a hint that narrows the search; second miss gets the full answer with its reason.
- Illegible handwriting in a photo: say which part you could not read and ask them to type that part.

## BITSAT multiple choice (Part 3)

Use the option craft in the question-tools section. BITSAT level means: one correct option of four, solvable in one to two minutes, needing the method plus a twist, with each wrong option the result of a common error.
